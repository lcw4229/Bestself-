# Texas A&M Spanish Placement Exam — Research Brief

_Compiled 2026-08-12. Sources listed at the bottom. TAMU's own `artsci.tamu.edu`
pages are egress-blocked from this environment, so a few details are drawn from
mirrored/secondary TAMU pages and TAMU-system schools; **verify the starred (★) items
directly on the official page before relying on them.**_

## What the exam is

- The **Spanish Placement Test** is run by TAMU's Department of Global Languages &
  Cultures (formerly Hispanic Studies). Any incoming student with prior Spanish
  knowledge (home or school) and **no college credit** in Spanish must take it before
  enrolling in a first Spanish course.
- It also serves as the basis for **credit by examination** — you can *test out of*
  courses and earn credit, not just get placed.
- Students with qualifying **AP** or **IB** (or other accepted) scores do **not** need
  the departmental placement test.

## Format ★

- **Delivered online through Canvas** (moved from in-person). Purchased through the
  **Texas A&M University Store** ("Placement Exam" option); a fee applies (~**$30**).
- **90 minutes.**
- Always includes a **grammar** component and a **reading** component.
- Structure is a **mix of multiple-choice and written** questions.
- **Some versions include a listening portion and an essay/writing portion.** Plan for
  both. (Writing portions make score posting take longer.)
- Scores post to the **Howdy portal** in **3–5 business days** (My Record →
  Degree Evaluation → "Credit By Examination"); longer if there's a writing section.

> Many schools in the TAMU system (and nationally) use **WebCAPE** — a *computer-
> adaptive* test of grammar + vocabulary + reading, ~20–30 min, no listening/speaking,
> one attempt. TAMU College Station's version is fuller (adds reading + possible
> writing, 90 min). **Prepare for the harder version**; if the real thing is shorter,
> you're over-prepared.

## What it's worth

- **Maximum credit is 14 hours**, covering **SPAN 101, 102, 201, 202**.
  - SPAN 101 — Beginning Spanish I
  - SPAN 102 — Beginning Spanish II
  - SPAN 201 — Intermediate Spanish I
  - SPAN 202 — Intermediate Spanish II
- "Acing" / "5 out of 5" here means: **place out of all four and claim the full 14
  hours** (highest placement the departmental exam grants).
- Course prereqs confirm the ladder: SPAN 201 needs 102 (C+) *or placement by exam*;
  SPAN 202 needs 201 (C+) *or placement by exam*.

## Scoring / cutoffs ★

- Exact score→course cutoff ranges are **not published**; the exam is adaptive/graded
  internally and the department maps your score to the highest course you place out of.
- Practical implication: there's no "passing bar" to memorize — **maximize raw grammar/
  reading accuracy** and the placement takes care of itself. Confirm current cutoffs
  with the department if they matter for a specific credit decision.

## The single highest-leverage fact for prep

Because it's **credit-by-exam and adaptive/graded**, the ceiling is set by
**intermediate-to-advanced grammar accuracy**. The distinctions that decide a top
placement, in rough order of test frequency:

1. **Preterite vs. imperfect** (incl. meaning-changing verbs)
2. **Subjunctive** — present *and* imperfect, all trigger families
3. **Ser vs. estar**
4. **Por vs. para**
5. **Object pronouns** (direct/indirect/double, placement, accents)
6. **Commands** (formal, tú irregulars, pronoun attachment)
7. Perfect tenses, future/conditional, gustar-type verbs, relative pronouns, agreement

See `01-curriculum.md` for the full study map and `02-practice-bank.md` for drills.
